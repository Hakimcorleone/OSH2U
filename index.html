How a rating is made

From registration to a grade that keeps moving

  1. Register

    The trainer submits credentials, practice history and two client references, and gets a workspace straight away.

    About 10 minutes

  2. Verify at source

    Every credential is checked with the body that issued it. Self-declared items that cannot be verified score zero.

    5 to 10 working days

  3. Collect stars

    Participants rate each session from a QR code, and answer three questions on how it was run. Only confirmed attendees count, and the trainer cannot edit a review.

    10 reviews before a grade

  4. Score it

    OSH2U works out the score from the evidence: stars 60%, then verified credentials, delivery and experience. No panel and no interview.

    Calculated, not voted on

  5. Publish and keep moving

    The trainer sees the grade 14 days before it goes live. Reviews keep arriving, so the grade is recalculated with them.

    Updates as evidence arrives

Rating methodology

The criteria are public so that any trainer can work out why they hold the grade they hold, and any employer can judge how much weight to put on it.

What a rating is, and is not

An OSH2U rating is an independent, forward-looking opinion on one question: how likely is this trainer to deliver competent, current and effective occupational safety and health training over the next 12 months?

It starts with the people who were in the room. Verified participant stars carry more weight than any other input, and four further assessments keep the grade honest.

It is not a regulatory approval, a licence, or a guarantee of any outcome. OSH2U is not affiliated with DOSH, NIOSH Malaysia or HRD Corp, and a rating does not replace any registration those bodies require. A rating rates the individual trainer, not the training provider they work for.

The scale

There are six published grades and no plus or minus notches in criteria v0.3. The score behind each grade is shown on the report, so movement within a grade is still visible.

Four assessments

Each assessment is scored from 0 to 100, and the weighted total is the rating score. Only evidence that OSH2U has verified counts: a credential that cannot be confirmed with its issuing body scores zero, however it is described on a CV.

Participant stars

Participant stars are the largest part of every grade, at 60%. At the end of a session the trainer shows a QR code, and each participant gives 1 to 5 stars and an optional comment. OSH2U collects the review directly and matches it to the attendance list, so only people who were in the room count, once per session.

The star rating on the register is the plain average of verified reviews from the last 24 months, shown to one decimal place with the number of reviews beside it. It is never scaled against other trainers. For the grade, the average out of 5 is converted to a score out of 100, so 4.4 stars becomes 88.

The attendance list comes from the employer or training provider that booked the session, not from the trainer. OSH2U sends the review link to everyone on that list, so a trainer cannot choose who gets asked. Reviews from a single session can never make up more than half of the 10 needed for a full rating.

  • A trainer needs 10 verified reviews before any grade is given. Until then the register shows Registered, with the stars collected so far.
  • Trainers cannot add, edit or remove reviews. OSH2U removes a review only when it is abusive or comes from someone who did not attend, and logs the removal.
  • Patterns that suggest pressure on participants, such as a run of identical five-star reviews within a minute, are investigated and can put the rating under review.

Built to grow trainers

OSH2U exists to raise the standard of OSH training, and that only works if trainers want to be on the register. So the system is built to encourage.

  • All six grades are published. AAA down to B, exactly as the score falls. Hiding the lower grades would make the higher ones mean less, and an employer needs to be able to tell them apart.
  • Problems are raised privately first. If a credential is about to lapse or stars are slipping, the trainer is told in their workspace and has 90 days to act before any public change.
  • Grades move both ways. A rating that could only rise would mean nothing to an employer. A grade can be revised down as the evidence changes, one step at a time, and the trainer is told privately first.
  • Every trainer gets a growth path. The workspace shows which assessment would lift the score most, and what each grade unlocks.

Outlook and review

An outlook flags the direction the evidence is moving over the next 12 months. It is either Positive or Stable, and it follows the trend in participant stars and the currency of credentials. It is not a promise of a change.

A rating is placed under review when a specific event could change it, for example a complaint being looked into or a required credential that has lapsed. The grade is not shown while the review is open. It closes within 90 days with the grade affirmed, revised or returned to Registered.

Stars on the register update as reviews arrive, and the grade is recalculated with them, up or down. Nobody overrides the arithmetic. A trainer sees the change in their workspace before it is published, and a grade that is about to fall is flagged to them first.

Independence and conflicts of interest

  • Trainers do not pay to be registered, verified or rated, and the workspace is free.
  • Commercial products such as the employer request service and optional workspace tools are run by a separate team, with no access to rating files and no way to change a grade.
  • No analyst rates a trainer they have worked with, trained under or competed against in the past three years.
  • There is no rating panel. The grade is calculated from the published weights, so the same evidence always gives the same grade and nobody can be talked into a better one.
  • Where OSH2U or any of its partners has a commercial relationship with a rated trainer, the report says so.
  • Criteria are versioned. Changes are published for consultation before they apply, and every report states the criteria version used.

Appeals and corrections

A trainer sees their grade 14 days before it is published. They may dispute the evidence behind it once per change, on facts only: a credential that was missed, a date that is wrong, a review from someone who did not attend. OSH2U rechecks the evidence and recalculates. Disagreement with the weights themselves is not grounds for a dispute, but the trainer may add a 150-word response that is published with the report.

A participant's star rating cannot be appealed, but a review that breaks the rules can be reported and will be checked against the attendance record.

Factual errors reported after publication are corrected within 5 working days and the correction is logged on the report.
